1. Product Background
Vietnam's e-commerce market has emerged as one of the fastest-growing in Southeast Asia. According to the Ministry of Industry and Trade, Vietnam's retail e-commerce market surpassed USD 25 billion in 2024, marking a 20% increase compared to the previous year — exceeding the earlier forecast of USD 22 billion made by Google, Temasek, and Bain & Company [1]. E-commerce now accounts for over 60% of Vietnam's digital economy, ranking third in the region behind Indonesia and Thailand [1]. According to YouNet ECI's full-year 2024 report, Shopee and TikTok Shop led the market with GMV of USD 9.3 billion and USD 3.8 billion respectively, together capturing approximately 93.6% of platform market share [2].
This explosive growth has drawn hundreds of thousands of small and medium-sized enterprises onto e-commerce platforms. In Quarter 2 2024 alone, Shopee recorded 261,000 active sellers, TikTok Shop had 113,000, Lazada had 104,000, and Tiki had 8,800 [3]. However, the rapid expansion has also intensified competition.
As order volumes surge, most Vietnamese SME sellers still rely on manual methods to manage their warehouse operations — a reality reflected in the fact that inventory inaccuracy remains one of the most critical warehouse challenges in Vietnam, primarily caused by human error during stock counts and order picking, leading to significant discrepancies between recorded and actual inventory levels [4]. Globally, 72% of SMEs experience unpredictable delivery times from suppliers, and SMEs carry the highest labour costs at an average of 27.7% of revenue — inefficiencies that compound when inventory is poorly managed [5]. 
These gaps highlight the urgent need for an affordable, accessible, and localised warehouse management solution tailored to the scale of Vietnamese SME e-commerce businesses. StockLite is developed to address this opportunity — providing a lightweight system with an Angular web admin dashboard and a Flutter mobile application.

Target customer profile.
StockLite targets Vietnamese SME e-commerce sellers that: (a) operate a single physical warehouse or stockroom; (b) sell primarily non-perishable, individually countable goods — fashion & accessories, consumer electronics & accessories, home & living, toys, stationery, and similar non-expiry FMCG; (c) process roughly 50–200 outbound orders per day; and (d) employ 2–10 warehouse staff.

This target range is a v1 segmentation assumption supported by recent Vietnamese operating evidence. For example, Enoda Home, a Vietnamese home-and-living e-commerce seller, reported that warehouse productivity increased to approximately 190 outbound orders per warehouse employee per day after standardising warehouse operations, and could reach approximately 210 orders per employee per day during peak periods. The case demonstrates that sellers of individually countable home-and-living products can face meaningful warehouse-execution pressure even with a small operational team. However, the published case does not disclose the seller’s total daily order volume, total warehouse headcount, or number of physical warehouses. Therefore, StockLite treats the 50–200 orders-per-day [11] and 2–10 warehouse-staff [11] range as a validated target hypothesis to be confirmed through pilot interviews and on-site observation.

For this segment, lot/expiry traceability, which is generally required for cosmetics, food, and pharmaceuticals, and inter-warehouse transfer are not part of daily operations. Excluding them (LI-02, LI-06) therefore removes complexity without removing capability the target user actually needs. Sellers that require batch/lot/expiry control, multi-site logistics, or substantially larger warehouse operations fall outside the v1 target segment and are addressed in the post-launch roadmap (Section 5.3).
Basis of the problem statement.
The need described in this section is currently evidenced by secondary and industry data (the quantified-evidence table above and the cited e-commerce/inventory statistics). Primary field validation — structured interviews and on-site observation with pilot SME sellers — has not yet been conducted and is defined as the first activity after the v1 feature freeze (Section 5.3). The team treats the current scope as a validated hypothesis to be confirmed with pilot users, not as a market-proven requirement set.

Built around a modular app structure, Odoo lets businesses choose the tools they need and add more as they grow.

StockLite focuses on this smaller MVP scope: QR-based warehouse operations, IoT scale verification, stock movement traceability, and a mobile workflow for warehouse staff.

3. Proposed Solution
StockLite is an internal warehouse management system designed for small and medium-sized e-commerce businesses in Vietnam. It addresses the operational gaps identified in Section 2 by replacing fragmented stock tracking methods such as spreadsheets, chat messages, and manual records with a single, structured environment for managing warehouse activities. The product focuses on improving stock accuracy, inbound and outbound traceability, and overall warehouse control.
For store owners and warehouse managers, StockLite provides clearer visibility over inventory movement, stock status, and warehouse performance, helping them reduce stock discrepancies and make better operational decisions. For warehouse staff, guided workflows help reduce manual mistakes during receiving, picking, packing, and stock checking. As a result, warehouse operations become more consistent, traceable, and easier to manage as the business grows.
The timing is appropriate because Vietnam’s e-commerce market continues to expand rapidly, while many SME sellers still lack affordable and localized warehouse management tools. StockLite offers a lightweight and easy-to-adopt solution that fits the practical needs of Vietnamese SME warehouses, making warehouse digitalisation more accessible without requiring complex enterprise-level systems.
4. Project Scope & Limitations
4.1 Major Features
ID
Feature Name
Description
Addresses Gap
FE-01
Real-Time Inventory Tracking
Enables warehouse staff and managers to monitor real-time stock levels by ProductVariant/SKU, warehouse, and internal storage location. Inventory is updated only through confirmed inventory transactions such as inbound confirmation, outbound shipment confirmation, return confirmation, stocktake adjustment, or manual adjustment with permission.
GAP-01
FE-02
Inbound / Outbound Management with Approval
Supports staff-created inbound and outbound warehouse documents with manager/system admin approval before execution.
 
Inbound: staff create an inbound document (selecting an existing or quick-created supplier), submit it for approval, scan QR/SKU after approval, split received goods into receiving groups if needed, attach IoT weight evidence, accept or override the suggested storage location, and confirm receiving. The received quantity per line may differ from the ordered quantity; a document may be fully received, partially received, or rejected. Goods identified as short, over, damaged, or failing quality checks are recorded with a discrepancy reason and routed to a quarantine (non-available) location instead of available stock. Inventory at the confirmed location is increased only after final receiving confirmation.
 
Outbound: staff create an outbound document (selecting a customer), submit it for approval. On approval the system reserves the required quantity so that available stock = on-hand − reserved, preventing two orders from committing the same stock. After approval, staff perform QR-based picking from the system-indicated source location, then packing, attach optional IoT weight evidence, and confirm shipment. Reserved stock is released if the document is cancelled. Inventory is decreased only after final shipment confirmation. Outbound documents are created manually by warehouse staff based on orders the seller has already confirmed on marketplace platforms (Shopee / TikTok Shop) or other sales channels. Staff key in customer name, phone, address, and order lines directly on the outbound document (StockLite keeps no customer master table — see FE-15). Marketplace order data is entered manually in v1; automated order pull via marketplace APIs is out of scope (LI-01) and deferred to the roadmap (Section 5.3).
GAP-01
FE-03
Low Stock Alerts
Automatically notifies the store owner when product quantities fall below a configurable minimum threshold, enabling proactive restocking.
GAP-01
FE-04
Inventory Reporting & Analytics
Provides daily, weekly, and monthly reports on inventory movement, stock turnover, low-stock products, inventory discrepancies, and inventory adjustment summaries to support data-driven inventory management decisions 
GAP-03
FE-05
Role-Based Access Control
Allows the store owner to assign distinct roles (System Admin, Warehouse Manager, Warehouse Staff) with granular permissions, protecting sensitive data and ensuring accountability.
GAP-01
FE-06
Product & Category Management
Supports creation and organisation of product master data, categories, units of measure, and ProductVariant/SKU records. ProductVariant is the stock-keeping unit used in inbound documents, outbound documents, stocktake, inventory, QR scanning, IoT weight verification, and reporting. StockLite does not store SKU directly on the parent Product entity.
GAP-01
FE-07 
QR Warehouse Operations 
Generates or stores QR code values for each ProductVariant/SKU and enables warehouse staff to scan QR codes during inbound receiving, outbound picking/packing, and stocktake workflows. QR scanning verifies that the correct SKU is being processed before any inventory-changing confirmation is allowed.
GAP-02
FE-08 
Flutter Mobile Warehouse App 
Provides a mobile-first Flutter application for warehouse staff to execute warehouse-floor tasks, including QR scanning, inbound receiving, put-away location confirmation, outbound picking/packing, stocktake counting, IoT weight log retrieval, and final operation confirmation.
GAP-02 
FE-09 
IoT Scale Verification 
Integrates an IoT scale hardware component (ESP32 microcontroller + HX711 amplifier module) to transmit physical weight data to the backend during receiving (Inbound) or dispatching (Outbound) processes. Inventory is strictly managed by item quantity (units), not by weight. The IoT scale serves purely as an automated verification checkpoint: the system calculates the expected total weight (Standard Weight of Variant × Quantity) and compares it with the actual physical weight from the scale to instantly detect discrepancies such as under-receiving, wrong item picking, or incorrect packaging before final confirmation. The IoT scale is a verification checkpoint only. It does not replace quantity counting and does not update inventory automatically. Weight verification operates within a configurable tolerance band (±5%) to absorb sensor noise and lightweight items, and its result is advisory: a staff member may override a flagged mismatch with a reason. Connection loss, multi-SKU weighing, and items with non-uniform unit weight are handled as defined exception cases in Report 3 (scenarios) and never block inventory confirmation by themselves. Inventory is always driven by confirmed item quantity, never by weight.
GAP-01 
FE-10 
Stocktake & Inventory Adjustment 
Allows managers to create stocktake sessions and assign counting tasks to warehouse staff. Staff perform physical counting by QR/SKU and location, while the system compares counted quantities with system quantities. Any discrepancy must be reviewed and approved by a manager before the system creates adjustment transactions and updates inventory. Counting is performed by QR/SKU and by storage location, so each count line records the location it was counted at. While a stocktake session is active for a location, inbound/outbound transactions on that location are controlled (locked or recorded separately) to keep the counted quantity comparable with the system quantity. Each stocktake adjustment is recorded as a document with the counting staff, the approving manager, timestamp, and before/after quantities, and staff may attach supporting evidence (e.g. a count-sheet photo) into the same shared document store.
GAP-01 
FE-11 
Inventory Transaction History 
Records every confirmed inventory-changing event, including inbound receiving, outbound shipment, inbound returns, return-to-supplier, stocktake adjustment, and authorized manual adjustment. Each transaction stores reference document, reference item, ProductVariant/SKU, location, before quantity, after quantity, timestamp, and user information for auditability.
GAP-03
FE-12
Rule-Based Inbound Bottleneck Warning 
The system evaluates inbound workload during document submission and manager/system admin approval. It checks planned inbound quantity, expected receiving date, warehouse/location capacity, and available staff workload to warn managers about possible receiving congestion before the warehouse floor starts processing goods. The warning is advisory and does not automatically cancel inbound documents.
GAP-01 / GAP-03 
FE-13
Put-away Location Suggestion
The system suggests suitable internal storage locations during inbound receiving after QR/SKU validation and before final receiving confirmation. Suggestions are calculated based on ProductVariant, product category, available location capacity, current occupancy, and location priority. Staff may accept the suggested location or override it with a reason. Inventory is increased at the confirmed location only after final receiving confirmation.

GAP-01/
GAP-02
FE-14
Warehouse & Location Management
Supports configuration of the single active warehouse and its internal storage structure: zones, racks/shelves, and slots/bins, each with a maximum capacity (MaxCapacity), current occupancy, allowed product category, and put-away priority. This master data is the foundation for inventory-by-location tracking (FE-01), put-away suggestion (FE-13), and the inbound bottleneck warning (FE-12).
GAP-01 / GAP-02
FE-15
Supplier Management
Maintains supplier master records used by inbound documents. A supplier can be deactivated but not deleted while still referenced by existing inbound documents. A new supplier may be quick-created directly inside the inbound document screen when goods arrive from a supplier not yet in the system. Customer information is not maintained as master data; instead, customer name, phone, and address are entered directly on each outbound document. Financial/accounting scope is excluded.
GAP-01
FE-16
Returns Management (Reverse Logistics)
Handles the reverse-logistics flows that were previously folded into FE-02, now managed as a dedicated feature with two distinct sub-functions. (1) Customer / Inbound Returns: staff create an inbound-return document for goods returned by a customer (optionally referencing the original outbound document) and submit it for manager/system admin approval; returned goods are then inspected, with items that pass inspection restocked to an available location and damaged or failed-QC items routed to a quarantine (non-available) location with a recorded return reason. Inventory increases only after final return confirmation, and the event is logged as an inbound-return transaction. (2) Return-to-Supplier: staff create a return-to-supplier document referencing the supplier (and optionally the original inbound document) and submit it for approval; after approval they perform QR-based picking of the goods to be sent back and confirm dispatch. Inventory decreases only after final confirmation, and the event is logged as a return-to-supplier transaction. The financial and analytical layers of reverse logistics (refunds, credit/accounts-payable, automated carrier pickup, and return-reason analytics) remain out of MVP scope per LI-07.

5. Expected Contributions & Next Steps
5.1 Business & Operational Impact
By digitising staff-created inbound and outbound workflows with manager/system admin approval, QR-based warehouse-floor operations, IoT scale verification, and inventory transaction records, StockLite is expected to reduce manual spreadsheet reconciliation and improve inventory accuracy for SME warehouse teams. The approval workflow prevents unauthorized inventory movement, while QR scanning and IoT weight evidence reduce receiving, picking, and packing mistakes. Put-away suggestions help staff place received goods in suitable internal locations, improving later picking efficiency. Rule-based inbound bottleneck warnings help managers detect overloaded receiving days before warehouse congestion occurs. StockLite also covers the core return flows through the dedicated FE-16 Returns Management feature — inbound returns and return-to-supplier — so that returned goods are inspected and either restocked or quarantined, keeping system stock aligned with physical stock.
5.2 User Experience Impact
Warehouse staff benefit from a guided mobile workflow for creating warehouse documents, scanning QR codes, receiving goods, confirming put-away locations, picking goods, counting stock, and attaching IoT weight evidence directly on the warehouse floor. Managers and system admins benefit from an approval-focused web dashboard where they can review inbound/outbound documents, approve or reject requests, monitor warnings, review discrepancies, and track inventory movement history. Store owners gain a real-time view of inventory without manually cross-referencing spreadsheets, chat messages, and paper documents. The Flutter app is execution-only and single-task-guided — staff perform one warehouse operation at a time, with all management, approval, and reporting kept on the Angular web admin. This split keeps the mobile UX simple and testable within the project timeline.
5.3 Planned Next Steps After Project Completion
Following successful v1 deployment and validation with pilot businesses, the planned next steps include:
- Offline mode for Flutter warehouse app.
- Google Play / App Store release after pilot validation.
- E-commerce platform API integration with Shopee, TikTok Shop, Lazada, and Tiki.
- Improved OCR for delivery documents.
- AI-driven demand forecasting after enough historical data is collected.
- Full batch/lot/expiry tracking with FIFO/FEFO support for businesses that require lot-level traceability.
